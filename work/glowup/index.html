GlowUp Eye Care

Playful XR exercises that help children stick with the eye training they need.

  • Northwestern capstone
  • 6 weeks
  • Individual thesis
  • Role: Research and Experience Design

Reframed a childhood eye-care problem from awareness to adherence, and designed XR exercises children would actually finish.

Problem
Children with early oculomotor difficulty are given eye exercises that are repetitive, hard to track and easy to abandon.
Role
Research and Experience Design, as an individual thesis.
Process
A six-week project: 4 weeks of research, including 6+ interviews with leading ophthalmologists, then a speculative XR exercise system built around child attention spans.
Outcome
A design strategy for child-friendly, therapy-adjacent interaction, with playful, trackable routines and support for caregivers at home.

Skip to the design

01

Children with early oculomotor challenges can struggle to stay engaged with repetitive eye exercises.

Children now spend long hours on close-focus digital tasks, while early signs of visual strain and oculomotor difficulty often go unnoticed.

I set out to explore how design could make therapeutic eye exercises more engaging and easier to sustain, for children with early oculomotor challenges and the caregivers who support them.

02

Families were given instructions, and very little else.

Over 4 weeks of research, including interviews with 6+ leading ophthalmologists and desk research, the same gap kept appearing.

Families are usually given exercise instructions, but little support for motivation, pacing or confidence that they are doing it right.

What families get

  • Exercise instructions
The gap GlowUp

What they need at home

  • Motivation
  • Pacing
  • Confidence they are doing it right

03

The real problem was adherence, not awareness.

Existing exercises fail because children abandon them, not because families don't know about them.

So the design had to balance clinical goals, safety and a child's attention span, turn specialist eye movement exercises into interactions that feel intuitive, and help caregivers track consistency and progress over time.

Reframed from Awareness to Adherence

Halfway there.

Children did not need to know more. They needed a reason to keep going.

04

I built each session around a child's attention span, not a clinic's schedule.

GlowUp Eye Care is a guided XR exercise system. Short, focused sessions train three skills, and difficulty ramps up gradually so sessions stay approachable while still reinforcing the therapeutic movements.

  1. Skill 1

    Tracking

    Following a moving target smoothly with the eyes.

  2. Skill 2

    Focus shifting

    Switching focus between something near and something far.

  3. Skill 3

    Eye-hand coordination

    Spotting a target, then reaching for it.

Tracking

05

Play and therapy became the same action.

Every game-like task is aligned to a specific visual movement goal, turning clinical repetition into a playful, trackable routine.

Caregivers get simple summaries that support continuity at home.

Reflection

This project built my fluency in designing for healthcare, for families and for the wide variation in how children develop.

It also convinced me that engagement design is critical to therapy, not a layer on top of it. I designed play to make repeated practice more engaging.
